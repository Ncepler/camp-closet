# Environmental Impact Data — Clothing Resale Savings

This document contains the environmental impact numbers for three clothing item types sold on our platform. Use these numbers ONLY — do not invent or search for additional statistics.

Numbers represent the impact of producing ONE new item. When a used item is resold instead, roughly all of this impact is avoided (minus a small offset for shipping, typically 0.1–0.5 kg CO₂ per domestic shipment).

---

## Per-Item Impact

### Cotton T-shirt
- Primary material: Cotton
- Typical weight: 200 g
- Water used: 2,700 liters
- Energy used: 11 kWh
- CO2 emitted: 7.0 kg

**What that actually means — the same resources could have:**
- Given one person drinking water for 900 days (almost 2.5 years)
- Filled 34 bathtubs
- Powered a refrigerator for about 2 weeks
- Driven a gas car roughly 18 miles
- Kept 100 water bottles filled every day for a month

Notes: WWF figure. Cotton farming is water-intensive — it covers only 2.4% of global farmland but uses 11% of the world's pesticides.

---

### Polyester Jersey
- Primary material: Polyester
- Typical weight: 180 g
- Water used: 50 liters
- Energy used: 35 kWh
- CO2 emitted: 5.5 kg

**What that actually means — the same resources could have:**
- Powered a laptop for about 3,500 hours (nearly 5 months of constant use)
- Driven a gas car roughly 22 miles
- Run a household LED light for 4 years straight
- Charged a smartphone over 3,000 times
- Kept a ceiling fan running nonstop for 2 months

Notes: Polyester is literally made from petroleum — every new jersey is a small amount of crude oil pulled out of the ground. It produces 2–3x more CO2 than cotton per kg of fabric.

---

### Sweatshirt / Hoodie
- Primary material: Cotton blend
- Typical weight: 500 g
- Water used: 6,000 liters
- Energy used: 25 kWh
- CO2 emitted: 15.0 kg

**What that actually means — the same resources could have:**
- Given one person drinking water for over 5 years
- Filled an entire backyard swimming pool halfway
- Powered an average home for a full day
- Driven a gas car roughly 38 miles (about the distance from NYC to Greenwich, CT)
- Run a washing machine for 250 loads of laundry

Notes: Sweatshirts have proportionally more fabric, dye, and finishing than a tee — the whole production footprint scales up with garment weight.

---

## Combined Total (if one of each is reused)

- Water saved: 8,750 liters (enough drinking water for one person for 8 years)
- Energy saved: 71 kWh (over 2 full days of powering a home)
- CO2 avoided: 27.5 kg (equal to driving roughly 70 miles)

---

## Platform Impact Formulas

To calculate total platform impact, multiply the number of each item resold by its per-unit savings:

- Total water saved (L) = (t-shirts × 2,700) + (jerseys × 50) + (sweatshirts × 6,000)
- Total energy saved (kWh) = (t-shirts × 11) + (jerseys × 35) + (sweatshirts × 25)
- Total CO2 saved (kg) = (t-shirts × 7.0) + (jerseys × 5.5) + (sweatshirts × 15.0)

## Relatable Conversions for Platform Totals

Use these to translate cumulative platform numbers into things people can actually picture:

- Days of drinking water for 1 person = total water saved ÷ 3
- Bathtubs of water = total water saved ÷ 80
- Olympic swimming pools = total water saved ÷ 2,500,000
- Miles of driving (gas car) avoided = total CO2 saved ÷ 0.4
- Trees' worth of annual CO2 absorption = total CO2 saved ÷ 21
- Homes powered for a day = total energy saved ÷ 30
- Smartphone charges = total energy saved × 100

## Display Guidance

- Round numbers DOWN slightly in the UI for credibility. Show "~2,500L" instead of "2,700L", "~5kg CO2" instead of "5.5kg", etc.
- Lead with water savings for cotton items — most dramatic number, most shareable comparison.
- Lead with CO2 savings for polyester jerseys — the water number is small, the carbon story is strong.
- For each item, show the raw number AND at least one "what this means" comparison side by side. The comparison is what makes it land emotionally.
- Rotate between different comparisons to avoid repetition — don't use "drinking water for X days" on every item.

## Supporting Context (for copy and storytelling)

- Extending a garment's life by just 9 months reduces its water, carbon, and waste footprint by 20–30%. (Ellen MacArthur Foundation)
- The fashion industry is responsible for approximately 8% of global greenhouse gas emissions. (UN / ILO)
- Cotton farming accounts for 24% of the world's insecticide use and 11% of pesticide sales despite covering only about 2.4% of global agriculture. (World Resources Institute)
- Producing 1 kg of virgin polyester generates roughly 14.2 kg of CO2. (Industry LCA data)
- Recycled polyester production saves 30–59% of the energy required for virgin polyester.
- The average American throws away roughly 81 pounds of clothing per year — most of it still wearable.

## Methodology Notes

- Figures are averages across published LCA (life cycle assessment) studies. Actual impact varies by origin country, local energy grid, and farming practice.
- Numbers represent cradle-to-gate impact (production only), not full lifecycle including use or disposal.
- "Savings from resale" assumes the used item substitutes for one new item that would otherwise be manufactured.
- "What this means" comparisons use EPA-standard equivalents (e.g., 0.4 kg CO2 per mile for an average gas car, 30 kWh per home per day) and common household reference points.
